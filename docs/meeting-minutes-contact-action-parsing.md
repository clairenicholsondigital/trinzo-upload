# Bounded contact-action parser corrections

Scope: the proven purpose/destination and recipient-boundary gaps in tasks 4A and 4B. These fixes describe syntax; they do not establish that two actions are equivalent or authorise combining their objectives.

## Supported boundary

For a direct contact instruction with a single explicit capitalised name, separate the recipient from the following purpose or task description. Supported leads are speak/talk/discuss/check/liaise/consult/meet/coordinate with `to` or `with`. Preserve complete multiword names rather than absorbing `to clarify ...` or `the supplier activities ...` into the recipient.

Normalise only case, whitespace, Unicode presentation and curly apostrophes for recipient comparison. Different full names are different recipients, even when their first names match. A short name is not automatically an alias for a longer one. This does not change the Settings people registry or owner attribution.

Within this construction, `to clarify`, `to avoid` and the other explicitly recognised verbal purpose markers do not introduce delivery destinations. Real spatial phrases remain available to the destination comparison. Complete explicit named locations are guarded: North Warehouse and South Warehouse are not equivalent because both contain `warehouse`; `at` locations are recognised within the contact construction too.

Unknown grammar retains the previous parser. In particular, this batch does not guess identities for recipient lists, lowercase groups, punctuated titles, lowercase name particles, quotations or questions. A contact with no explicit following purpose/topic boundary also retains the old path: a sequence of capitalised words could be a long name or an implicit topic. It is not a universal natural-language parser or a complete solution for all possible forms of tasks 4A/4B.

## Preservation and evidence

All action wording, objectives, ownership, timing, citations, flags and restoration metadata remain unchanged by parsing. Existing containment, operation, scope, reviewer and source-verification checks remain in force. Fixing a false destination conflict does not allow deleting the registration/project-plan actions: their distinct objectives and protected details still prevent either action covering the other.

Generic checks exercise wrong recipients sharing a purpose, distinct full names sharing a first name, spelling/whitespace variants, long names, unknown grammar, real locations, differing owners/dates, reviewer protection, metadata-preserving literal duplicates and independent reconciliation vetoes.

Offline comparisons on five archived action lists (53 actions total; the archived Parking list has zero actions) retain exactly the same merge results with normal comparison and with every similarity forced to 1. The actual saved registration pair loses its false destination conflict but remains two separate actions, with operation/identifier/alternative requirements still blocking both containment directions. This is local saved-output replay, not a fresh generation or provider test. The inspected 49-draft backup has no active reconciliation receipts to compare; existing generic receipt/recovery tests provide that coverage.

No transcript-specific production rules, new model calls, Microsoft configuration edits, broader semantic-merge activation, citation-limit changes or existing draft rewrites. Combined multi-objective action synthesis, complete action-operation parsing and citation allocation (4C–4E) remain open, as do the discussion citation tasks and cross-batch referee work.
